# Decisions

Each decision, dated, with its reason. Newest last. What each session did is in [BUILD-LOG.md](BUILD-LOG.md).

## 2026-10-07 · the first working session (the founding)

1. **The intent comes first, and alone.** The repository's first commit is [INTENT.md](INTENT.md) and nothing else, exactly the text the owner approved. *Why:* everything after it is judged against it, so it has to exist before anything it would judge.
2. **The name is stringline.** *Why:* the owner's choice.
3. **The record is kept in two folders.** The working notes, the owner's words and review reports stay in a private folder. This repository carries a build log written for strangers, which is checked against the private notes in both directions. *Why:* what is shown is written for the reader, and the check keeps the public account from dropping a decision or a mistake.
4. **A check, not a promise, keeps private words out.** A local pre-push check scans every file, commit message, author line and tag being pushed against a private word list and refuses the push on any hit. It was shown to refuse planted words in a scratch copy before the first real push. *Why:* private stays private because a check refuses the push, not because a sentence says so.
5. **Both GitHub repositories, this one and its private sibling, are private** until the owner makes this one public. *Why:* the owner decides when it is ready to be seen.
6. **Commits carry a GitHub no-reply address**, an address GitHub provides so that the author's real email is not published. *Why:* no private email address enters the public history.
7. **No AI agents run inside the product yet, so none are designed yet.** In the way I build, designing the agents a product ships with is its own step, with its own document. That step waits until this build ships an agent. *Why:* there is nothing yet for that step to work on, and writing its document early would be a document about nothing.
8. **An earlier tool of the owner's, kept in its own private repository, stays private and outside this build.** Whether it is ever brought in or made public is not decided. *Why:* the owner paused it before this build began. The intent lists it under "not decided, on purpose".
9. **Nothing in a working session contacts anyone.** *Why:* anything sent to a person is the owner's own act.

## 2026-10-07 · the second working session (seeing and naming)

10. **The public log carries the build; upkeep stays private and is counted.** Every decision and every wrong turn about the build itself gets a line in the public log. Upkeep of the owner's other work (keeping shared notes tidy, the state of other work, the lists and logs he keeps for himself) is kept private, and each session's entry says in one line how many such items were kept private and why. The reader that checks each close confirms every private item is either in the log or in that count. The founding entry was written before this rule and stands as written. *Why:* publishing a line for every decision pulled the owner's workspace upkeep into a log meant for a stranger, which is what the owner learned from the founding ([LEARNED.md](LEARNED.md)). The count keeps the two-way check honest without the clutter.
11. **Taking any word off the private word list waits for the owner's word.** *Why:* a word taken off is a word the push check stops refusing. The founding took one off so that a pointer in these files could pass; that call should have been the owner's.
12. **The README stays signed with the owner's name.** *Why:* the owner's ruling; the founding had left it open for him.
13. **Questions for the owner come as one line; everything else the session decides.** Anything that waits for his word comes as what is recommended, why, and what happens on his yes. Everything else is decided, recorded with its reason, and told to him after. *Why:* the owner's ruling: recommendations, not menus. A question that reading or measuring can answer is not his to answer.
14. **The intent stays as it is for now.** Two phrases a cold reader could not follow get plain words from this session, and the owner rules any added line at the next one. *Why:* the intent changes only by his word.
15. **Each session's log entry is its own file, beside a one-screen front page.** [BUILD-LOG.md](BUILD-LOG.md) now holds the rules and the list of entries; the founding entry moved, byte for byte, to its own file. *Why:* every file here fits on one screen, and a second entry would have pushed the log past it. The entries sit in the top folder, not a subfolder, because the founding entry's links were written from there and must keep working without editing it.
16. **The first tool is a quote checker for the owner's own words; the runner-up is a done-list checker.** The quote checker compares each quote of a person against that person's typed messages and gives one of four outcomes: exact, corrected, absent, or can't check. Pulling the typed messages out of a session's record comes after it. *Why:* the intent names this tool first. The one quote checker that exists in the owner's private work reports what it could not check as missing, the very collapse this build exists to refuse, so the first tool shows his practice improving its own instrument in the open. The runner-up's original is the most portable, and its founding mistake is already in this log.

## 2026-10-07 · the third working session (map, order, spec)

17. **The intent's two unclear phrases each get one plain line after them, in the owner's words.** *Why:* two cold readers could not follow them; the intent changes only by his word, and he gave it.
18. **One public file, [WORDS.txt](WORDS.txt), holds the owner's typed lines that INTENT.md and LEARNED.md quote, exactly as typed, lines only.** *Why:* the quote checker then has real work in public from its first run (intent goal 3): each quote comes back exact or corrected, or is caught.
19. **The private-word check stops a push; every other check and tool reports.** The quote checker's "can't check" never stops work. Whether a given tool may stop the work is decided per tool, once its run log shows what it catches. *Why:* the owner's ruling, closing a question the intent had left open.
20. **The map is public ([MAP.md](MAP.md)); the specification is a one-screen front page ([SPEC.md](SPEC.md)) and five part files.** *Why:* both are about the build, not the owner's other work; every file fits on one screen.
21. **Everything runs on Python 3.11 or later with the standard library only, plus git.** *Why:* a stranger installs a tool on a clean machine and it works; the owner's instruments are Python and the earlier manuals' checks are rewritten in it, one runtime beats two, and the stamps and log name commits.
22. **The manual's source is a folder of typed records, one per file, and it is the only list of tools.** The README and the agent instructions are written from it and checked against it. *Why:* cards and check scope derive by key instead of from a hand-kept list, which was the drift cause in the owner's earlier manuals; the intent commits to one source for the site, the README and the agent's brief.
23. **English, one text per field.** *Why:* the reader is a stranger in one language; the shape leaves room for a second.
24. **The run log is one file per run, and every row says planted or real; a card's "what it has caught" is read from real rows only.** *Why:* no original log tells a test from a real run, so none can answer whether its tool ever caught anything real.
25. **The quote checker takes plain text of typed lines, not a session record.** *Why:* it stands alone on any machine; pulling lines out of a record comes later and writes the same shape.
26. **No separate package is specified for the manual pattern this build recreates (one source held as data, one card per tool, checks that keep it current).** *Why:* the repository is the install and the product at once, and nothing is sold.
27. **Code files and run rows are not bound by the one-screen rule; pages and records are.** *Why:* a tool split to fit a screen is worse code; the check says what it does not read.
28. **What a reader saw near the owner's reading limit is upkeep of his private notes: kept private and counted, never described here.** *Why:* the owner's ruling, applying the record rule.
29. **Whether a run was planted or real is set by whoever starts it, never by where a file sits.** A self-test is planted; a person's plant says so with a flag and a note; everything else a person runs is real. *Why:* the intent asks for faults planted where they would really arrive, among real files, and such a plant must still never count as a catch.
30. **Merges never rewrite history.** *Why:* every stamp and every run row names a commit; squashing or rebasing would orphan them all.
31. **The public copy of the private-word check is a check, not a tool.** It has no card and no run log, and claims no install (reason replaced by #37 at the sequel session). *Why:* which tools come next is the owner's call; the copy shows the mechanism and guards nothing here.
32. **Each line of [WORDS.txt](WORDS.txt) is the quoted span as the owner typed it, never the message around it**, and every close appends his typed learning there after LEARNED.md is written. *Why:* the owner's ruling on the shape; the append gives the checker a new real line each session and tests the session's transcription of his words.
33. **The build deepens its three tracks before release with four items: the extractor beside the checker, the push scanner as public code, a workflow over every test and planted fault, and the done-list checker as a hook.** Placed on the map now, specified at a further session. *Why:* the owner's ruling: the target is code a reviewer can read.

## 2026-10-07 · the third session's sequel (S3b: specifying the four placed items)

34. **The four items placed at the third session are each their own one-screen specification part, and [SPEC.md](SPEC.md) lists nine.** *Why:* every file fits on one screen, and a placed item with no done-when and no twin of its own is a claim a workflow could read green while running nothing.
35. **The extractor is the second half of the quote-checking tool: the same card, the same log id, a `caught` that is always empty, and the card's sentence counts its runs apart from the checker's.** *Why:* the owner's ruling names both halves as one tool, and one card with one log id is this session's reading of it; a producer catches nothing, and saying so is truer than inventing a catch for it; a sentence that counted its runs among those that could have caught would be the blind read the card exists to refuse.
36. **A run on material outside this repository leaves one content-free row in this repository's log: the phrase "outside the repository" for its paths, counts and item numbers for what it caught or could not see, never text; and a tool writes to the log of the repository that holds it, whatever folder it is run from.** *Why:* a path into a person's machine and a line of his private material are private; the card reads only this repository's log, so a row written anywhere else would never reach it; the owner's real records and lists stay private, and only counts reach the public record, which is what the map's after-release act already said.
37. **The private-word scanner is the guard's fifth check and public code, with a sample list, a sample hook and the words to install the hook in one's own repository; it is still a check, not a tool, with no card and no log, and this replaces the reason in #31.** *Why:* the owner's ruling, and decision #19: the private check stops a push; every other check reports. Showing a mechanism without saying how it installs is not readable; whether it becomes a tool with a card is the owner's call, since which tools come next waits for his word.
38. **The test runner finds every self-test in the tree; the workflow keeps no list of tests.** *Why:* a list in the workflow and a test in the tree are two hand-kept copies, and their drift is a test nothing runs, the most repeated gap in the originals this build recreates.
39. **The done-list checker's hook is armed by a settings entry naming the list, acts on the end of a main session's turn only, and writes no run row.** *Why:* arming from inside a session is the private instrument's step and is not needed when the entry names the list; in the owner's practice the hook has been seen to reach a session in its own terminal and never a background seat, so the public tool claims only what was seen; a row at every turn end would flood the log in a session where nothing commits.
40. **A fourth build lane, D, builds the done-list checker after lane C's log module merges; the merge order is C's module, A, C's tool, B, D; lane B moves to Claude Opus 5.5 and lane C's budget grows.** *Why:* lane D needs only the module and the card's keys; the runner finds D's tests without an edit to B; the scanner reads git's plumbing on a push and proves its own hook, which is more than four small checks; lane C gained a second half.
41. **A spoken quote is the quote checker's own fifth count, out of its exit code.** *Why:* it is a standing declaration, not a blind spot; counted as can't check it would keep the workflow red from the first page that quotes speech, and a red that never clears teaches readers to ignore red.
42. **A card may carry a `catching` key naming the outcomes of the half of its tool that can catch; the tool prints them for `--catching`, and check 2 compares the two.** *Why:* the card's sentence counts the two halves apart (#35), and the names must live in one place, the corpus, not by hand in the renderer.
43. **Every self-test accepts `--no-log` and ends with the line `selftest: PASS` or `selftest: FAIL`.** *Why:* one runner fires every test with one command and reads one line; a test that rejected the flag or ended another way would read can't tell for good.
44. **The push scanner folds look-alike letters by a hand-kept substitution table before matching, with a plant of its own, as the approved guard spec required; its plants number fifteen, the committer line its own.** *Why:* the owner's ruling: the public scanner's first draft dropped the case with no decision, while its own reason cites the founding's wrong turn 3, which names look-alike letters; the table is small by design, so a disguise outside it passes and the header says so.
